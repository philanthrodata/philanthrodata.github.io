Mission

Helping the people who help others

PhilanthroData's mission is to give under-resourced nonprofits enterprise-grade data and analytics capability, and the skills to keep it running on their own.

Most nonprofits will never have the budget for a director-level data hire. PhilanthroData gives them that level of experience anyway, and leaves them able to keep it going on their own.

Why PhilanthroData exists

The gap

Too big for spreadsheets, too small for a data team

Plenty of nonprofits have outgrown spreadsheets. They run a CRM, a website, an email platform and maybe a dashboard or two. What they can't justify is a full-time data person to make those systems work together, so the numbers stay unreliable and reporting takes far more staff time than it should.

Large organizations solve this by hiring a data team. PhilanthroData brings that same experience to organizations that can't, one practical problem at a time.

How the model works

Paid work funds help for everyone else

01 · Sliding-scale engagements

Organizations pay what fits their size

Rates are tied to annual budget, so larger organizations pay more and smaller ones pay less, all well below typical consulting rates. See pricing

02 · Subsidized support

A third of billing funds free help

A third of everything I bill goes toward subsidized work for small nonprofits that couldn't otherwise afford this kind of help.

03 · Free tools

Common fixes become free tools

When the same problem keeps coming up, I turn the fix into a tool any nonprofit can use. The first ones check donor and contact data for lapsed donors and duplicates, right in your browser. More will follow as patterns emerge. Try the free tools

Built to hand off

Success means you don't need me

Every engagement ends with a handoff. One staff member gets a monthly checklist and a standing AI prompt that take about 30 minutes a month, so the work keeps paying off after I leave. Clients shouldn't depend on PhilanthroData, or any consultant, to keep their data working.
