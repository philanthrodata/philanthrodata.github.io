Before you export

A household is not a duplicate.

  1. Keep the record ID. You will need it to find each record again in your CRM.
  2. Include email status if you have it. Subscribed, unsubscribed, or an opt-out checkbox lets us flag copies that could email someone who said no.
  3. Leave out what you don’t need. Names, email, phone, address, and a last activity date are enough. Skip notes and giving history.

No sign-in, uploads, or changes to your systems. People who share an address or a family email stay separate unless their names match.

Matching rules, limits, and how to validate

Strong duplicates. Exact personal email plus exact first and last name. Probable duplicates use nickname or similar-name evidence with email, phone, organization, or a full street address. A shared address, ZIP code, or email domain alone never matches two people, so spouses and housemates stay separate.

Review flags. Shared inboxes (info@, office@), the same email with different first names (often a couple sharing one address), Jr./Sr. conflicts, and repeated record IDs need a person to look. Nicknames (Bob and Robert) are a guide, not proof.

Found in both. When the same person appears in two different systems, such as your CRM and your email list, the pair is listed separately and never counted as a duplicate. That overlap is normal; it shows which contacts your systems share.

Unsubscribe conflicts. If you map an email status or opt-out column, a duplicate group where one copy is unsubscribed and another is not is flagged. Emailing the other copy could reach someone who asked you to stop.

Recency. Each source row is counted, including linked and candidate records. A populated Last Activity Date takes precedence over Updated At / LastModifiedDate. Blank activity dates fall back to modified dates. Invalid populated activity dates are flagged rather than replaced. Dates accept YYYY-MM-DD with optional time, or US MM/DD/YYYY; future dates are separate. Date-only age uses calendar days.

Coverage and scale. Blocking narrows comparisons using email, person ID, phone, name + company, and name + street / locality. Fuzzy matching needs a common first initial and first three last-name characters within a company, so some typos are missed. Blocks over 250 rows are skipped; runs stop at 1.5 million comparisons or 100,000 evidence pairs. Any such limit is shown as partial coverage. “No candidates” is not proof that a database is clean.

Validation. The sample files have known answers, and a green bar confirms the tool found them. On your own data, spot-check ten groups before merging anything. Email syntax is checked here; whether an address can actually receive mail is not.
