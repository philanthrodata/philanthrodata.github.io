Using AI Without the ChaosA simple system for nonprofit teams

A free guide from PhilanthroData · Chad Priest

Most nonprofits don't need more AI tools. They need a simple habit: keep what you know in a few short files, and use AI chats as disposable workspaces that read from them.

Why AI feels inconsistent

AI tools remember far less than people expect. Most start each new chat knowing nothing about your organization, and even within one chat they lose track as it grows long.

That's why the same question can get a great answer on Monday and a generic one on Friday. The fix isn't a better tool. It's giving every tool the same short, current reference files.

The one idea: your files are the memory, and chats are scratch paper. This works with any AI tool your team uses, today or five years from now.

1Set up one shared folder

Create one folder in the drive your team already uses, such as Google Drive or SharePoint, and keep it to short, plain documents anyone can open.

AI Reference/
  About Us.doc          mission, programs, audiences, tone of voice
  How We Write.doc      style rules, words to use and avoid
  Prompts We Reuse.doc  prompts that worked well, ready to copy
  Projects/
    Spring Appeal 2027 – Brief.doc
    Website Refresh – Brief.doc
    Volunteer Program – Brief.doc
  Archive/              finished projects
  • Keep each file to about one or two pages. Short files are quick to paste into any tool.
  • Name one person as the keeper of About Us and How We Write.
  • Never put passwords or donor personal information in this folder.

2Write a one-page brief for each project

A brief lets any tool, or any new staff member, pick up a project without the backstory. Copy this template and fill it in.

# [Project name] – Brief
Last updated: [date]

Goal: What we're trying to achieve, in one sentence.
Audience: Who this is for.
Key facts: Dates, numbers, names the AI must get right.
Decisions so far:
- [date] what we decided and why
Open questions:
- what still needs an answer, and who owns it
Avoid: Topics, words or claims we never use.

3Treat each chat as one task

A chat works best when it has one job, a clear start, and a clear end.

  1. Start: name the chat after the task. Paste in About Us and the project brief, then say exactly what you want back ("a 150-word appeal email for lapsed donors").
  2. Work: stay on that one task. If a new topic comes up, write it down and save it for a fresh chat.
  3. Finish: ask, "Summarize what we decided and anything I should add to the brief." Paste that into the brief, and save the final result in your folder.

That last step is what makes the next chat smarter than this one.

4Know when to start a new chat

Start fresh when any of these happens. Ask for a summary first, then paste it into the new chat.

  • You're moving to a different task or project.
  • The AI forgets or contradicts something you told it earlier.
  • Answers get vaguer, repeat themselves, or ignore your instructions.
  • The chat has gone more than about 20 to 30 back-and-forths.
  • You pasted a long document and you're done with it.
  • The tool warns that the conversation is getting long.

5Use AI efficiently

AI tools measure work in tokens, small pieces of words. One token is about three-quarters of a word, so 1,000 tokens is roughly a page and a half.

The key fact: every time you send a message, the AI rereads the entire conversation, including everything you pasted. A long chat costs more with every turn, and paid plans' usage limits are based on it.

  • Paste the relevant section, not the whole 40-page report.
  • Use a brief instead of a long chat history. A one-page brief replaces dozens of turns.
  • Ask for the length you need: "5 bullets" or "under 150 words."
  • Turn off extras such as deep thinking, web search, or connected apps when a task doesn't need them.
  • Use the lighter model in your tool for simple rewording and formatting.

6Pick the right tool and protect donor data

Match the tool to the stakes, and keep personal information out unless you know where it goes.

TaskWhat to useWhy
Grant narratives, board reports, strategyYour tool's strongest model, with deep thinking onQuality matters most here
Emails, social posts, newslettersYour tool's everyday modelGood quality, lower usage
Rewording, formatting, summariesThe lightest model availableLittle reasoning needed
Anything needing current factsA tool with web search turned onOtherwise answers may be out of date
Donor lists, client records, anything with names or contact detailsRemove or replace personal details first, or use an approved business accountProtects donors and your reputation

Before your team relies on any AI tool, check two things in its settings: whether your conversations can be used to train its models, and who in your organization can see shared chats. Many providers offer nonprofit pricing; TechSoup is a good place to start.

Monthly 15-minute checklist

  • Update the "Last updated" date and open questions on each active brief
  • Move finished projects to Archive
  • Add any prompt you reused twice to Prompts We Reuse
  • Check About Us for new programs, staff, or numbers
  • Delete old chats whose work is captured in a brief
