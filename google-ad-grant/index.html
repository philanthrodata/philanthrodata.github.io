Free guide · Google Ad Grants

Up to $10,000 a month in free Google search ads

Google gives eligible nonprofits up to $10,000 a month to spend on Google Search ads. Applying is free and takes a few weeks. Here's who qualifies, what to have ready, how the application works today, and the rules that keep the grant active once you have it.

Get the printable checklist Jump to the steps

Who qualifies

In the United States, your organization needs to:

  • Be a registered charity. For most U.S. organizations that means 501(c)(3) status. If your IRS determination is still pending, wait until it arrives.
  • Pass verification by Goodstack, Google's verification partner, through a Google for Nonprofits account.
  • Have a working website on your own domain, served over HTTPS, with real content about your mission and programs.

Not eligible: government entities, hospitals and health care organizations, and schools, colleges and universities. Charitable foundations attached to a hospital or school can qualify.

Step by step

How to apply

Google has simplified the process. Older guides mention a pre-qualification form and creating your own Google Ads account. Neither applies now.

1. Check your eligibility and website

Time: about an hour

  1. Confirm you're a registered charity and not an excluded type of organization.
  2. Make sure your website uses HTTPS and has substantial content about your work.
  3. Read Google's Ad Grants website policy and fix anything that doesn't meet it.

2. Request a Google for Nonprofits account

Time: 2 to 14 business days for verification

  1. Sign in to Google for Nonprofits with your organization's Google account and click Get started.
  2. Enter your EIN, legal name and address exactly as the IRS has them.
  3. Goodstack verifies your status. Upload your determination letter if it asks.
  4. Watch for email from Goodstack, including your spam folder.

3. Activate Google Ad Grants

Time: several business days for Google's review

  1. In Google for Nonprofits, click Get started under Google Ad Grants.
  2. Enter the website you'll advertise.
  3. Watch the short program video and confirm you've watched it.
  4. Click Submit activation request.

Don't create a Google Ads account yourself at any point. Google provides the grant account when you're approved.

4. Set up the account before you spend

Time: a few hours, then a monthly check

  1. Set up conversion tracking for meaningful actions such as donations, sign-ups and volunteer forms.
  2. Build campaigns around specific pages and the phrases people search, not single words.
  3. Use a conversion-based bid strategy such as Maximize conversions.
  4. Add at least two sitelinks, and target the area you actually serve.
After approval

The rules that keep the grant active

Google can pause an account that falls out of line. Most of these take a few minutes a month to check.

Where applications get stuck

  • The name doesn't match. "Friends of the Park" and "Friends of the Park, Inc." can count as different organizations. Use the exact IRS name.
  • The login belongs to one person. When that person leaves, the grant goes with their account. Use a shared organizational address and add a second admin.
  • A Google Ads account was created by hand. It isn't the grant account and can cause activation problems.
  • Nobody tracks conversions. Without them, smart bidding has nothing to aim for and the account falls out of compliance.
  • The account runs on autopilot. Click-through rate drifts down slowly. A 15-minute monthly check catches it early.

Plenty of grant accounts use only a small part of the $10,000. Results come from sending people to specific pages that answer what they searched for, not from sending everyone to the homepage or donate page.
